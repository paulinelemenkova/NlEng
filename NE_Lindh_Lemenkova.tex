\section{Study objectives}
On behalf of Geomind KB, SMA and CEMEX, SGI has performed laboratory determinations and evaluation of strength properties on stabilized clay from Norvik, Nynäshamn municipality. The testing concerned both mixing tests for depth stabilization and mixing tests for jet injection. The aim was to test different binder combinations for depth stabilization and for jet injection.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Recipe for KC Pillar Stabilization}
To evaluate binder combinations for depth stabilization, an experimental set-up called simplex lattice was used (Montgomery, 1996; Myres and Montgomery, 1995). An experimental set-up according to the simplex lattice with three binders means that the experimental room consists of a triangle with pure binders in the corners and a mixture of two binders along the triangle's stripes. Inside the triangle is a combination of all three binders. Each point on the triangle represents 100\% binder, but the mutual ratio between the binders varies depending on where in the triangle the test point is located. The tests were carried out according to SGI's laboratory quality handbook, document 29a, rev c. Three different binders have been used, cement, lime and lime kiln dust (English; lime kiln dust, LKD). The cement used in the tests has been a Cem II/A and the lime is a burnt lime, named Ql in the figures (from the English 'quicklime').

From these three binders, nine different binder combinations have been tested. The mixing amounts corresponded to 80, 100 and 120 kg of binder per cubic meter of clay. The curing times for the reference samples (cement/lime 50/50) were determined to be 7, 14, 21 and 28 days. Some extra specimens were produced for printing at 80 days. The choice of 80 days is based on the fact that the samples were stored at 7°C. At this storage temperature and a curing time of 80 days, this corresponds to 560 degree days, which corresponds to 28 days of storage at 20°C.

The experimental setup was performed as a three-factor Simplex lattice with a limitation of the LKD content. The restriction meant that the LKD content was maximized to 50\%. The test surface in a three-factor Simplex lattice is structured as a triangle. The purpose of using this type of statistical trial planning is to minimize the number of trials while maintaining statistical significance and to ensure that both positive and negative interactions between the binder components are detected. Each point on the surface corresponds to 100\% binder. The selected trial points are in this case marked with blue circles, see Figure \ref{Fig:01}. The experimental design according to a restricted simplex lattice is shown in Figure \ref{Fig:01} where the restriction consists in maximizing the share of LKD to 50\%. The design was used for the different amounts of binder 80, 100 and 120 kg of binder per $m^3$ of clay. The blue rings correspond to tested binder combinations. The full circle represents both test point and extra reference samples, Figure \ref{Fig:01}.

\end{figure}

For this, extra reference tests were carried out which were to be used for calibration of seismic measurement in relation to compressive strength at 7 and 14 days, see Figure \ref{Fig:02}. The experimental design for the amount of binder 100 kg of binder per $m^3$ of clay was supplemented with internal points, cf. Figure  \ref{Fig:01}. Each trial point was performed as a double trial. The corners of the triangle represent 100\% of only one binder. The center of the triangle corresponds to 33\% of the three different binders. The points on the lower border of the triangle correspond to either a pure binder, (cf. CEM II or Ql) or alternatively a mixture of 50\% CEM II and 50\% Ql. Three different Simplex experiments were performed. These were equivalent to 80, 100 and 120 kg of binder per m3 of clay.

\subsection{Seismic measurements}
The seismic measurement was performed as a free-free resonant column measurement of the P-wave velocity. The measurements were to be used on all specimens in a Simplex test. By producing extra specimens that were used as reference specimens, i.e. determination of both P-wave velocity and shear strength at 7 and 14 days, a reference to the p-wave velocity of the remaining specimens would be used for a correlation to the shear strength of the samples. This type of correlation has previously been used for surface stabilized specimens with good results (Rydén et al, 2006).

The difference in execution for this project was here that the test cases were not completely filled with material. The empty sleeves had eigenfrequencies close to the eigenfrequencies of the test bodies. This meant that the sleeves with samples in generated oscillation modes that deviated significantly from the natural frequency of the sample. As the natural frequency of the sleeves dominated, it was these frequencies that were measured, cf. Appendix 1. This meant that none of the measurements on test bodies in sleeves could be evaluated. However, measurements carried out on deformed and trimmed specimens gave good results.

\subsection{Binder recipe for jet injection}
Testing of the binder recipes has been carried out partly by SGI and partly by CEMEX. The tests carried out by CEMEX comply with EN 196-1 and EN 196-3 and involve, among other things, that some binder has been mixed with three parts standard sand and half a part water (vbt = 0.5). In the tests carried out at SGI, only water and binder have been mixed and in parts of the tests clay has also been mixed in. This leads to significant differences in compressive strength. Furthermore, the test performed by CEMEX is done on prisms and the test performed by SGI is done on cylinders with a diameter of 50 mm and a height of 100 mm.

In the experiments carried out at SGI, binder and water were mixed in a dough mixer for 5 minutes until a homogeneous mixture was obtained. The samples were then cast into test cases. In cases where clay was added, the mixing time was increased to 20 minutes in order to obtain a homogeneous mixture. After one to three days of curing, the specimens were de-shaped and trimmed to the correct length. Due to the water separation, the specimens were trimmed mostly on the upper part as this was weaker. No water separation could be observed in the samples containing clay. The initial tests were carried out by CEMEX in accordance with the current cement standard according to SS-EN 196-1 (Anon, 2005a) and SS-EN 196-3 (Anon, 2005b). The testing performed by CEMEX and included in this report is shown in Table 1.

For the evaluation of binders for jet pillar reinforcement, cement, lime kiln dust (LKD) and cement kiln dust (CKD) were used. The cement used in the tests was a CEM II/A-M. For binder recipes A and B a water binder number (vbt) of 1.0 was used and for recipes C and D a vbt of 1.2 was used. For the tested binder combinations, experiments were carried out with the mixing of clay. The total clay content was 17, 30, 37.5 and 58 percent by weight. For binder recipe D, no clay admixtures were carried out due to difficulties in obtaining a homogeneous admixture of clay in the sample and a low compressive strength without clay admixture. The tested binder combinations are reported in Table 2.

The test performed on the test specimens was the measurement of compression wave velocity and compressive strength. Unlike the measurements performed on stabilized soil for K/C column reinforcement, all the seismic measurements were performed on specimens without pipes. 

In addition to the above tests, the viscosity data were determined according to the Marsh cone on recipes A to D without the addition of clay. The mixed clay recipes gave no results in the Marsh cone measurements because no material passed the funnel. Furthermore, temperature measurements were carried out according to the thermos method on Cem II with a vct of 1.0 and Cem II/LKD (50/50) with a vbt of 1.0. Steel thermoses with a liquid volume of 0.5 l were used for the experiment.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Results from K/C pillar the recipes}
\subsubsection{Strength determination}
The report shows good interaction effects between lime, cement and LKD for depth stabilization purposes. Most of the binder combinations met the requirements for a minimum shear strength of 200 kPa after 28 days of storage at 7\degree. Furthermore, tests carried out show that two out of four tested recipes for jet injection passed the requirement of 3 MPa after 28 days of storage even with a clay mixture of 58\%.

In the testing of binder combinations, a reference recipe of Cem II and quicklime was used in a mutual ratio of 50/50. Evaluated shear strength for the reference samples at different curing times is reported in Figure \ref{Fig:03}.

The result is presented as a response surface. For a binder quantity corresponding to 80 kg/m3, the result is reported in Figure \ref{Fig:04}. The result shows a clear positive interaction between the various components (Cement, quicklime and LKD) in a binder combination. Positive interaction means that the components generate a higher strength together than would be expected from a linear regression between the effects of the single-slip binders. The model has an explanation rate of 95\%, i.e. the model explains 95\% of the variation in shear strength.

\subsubsection{Seismic measurements}

The seismic measurements were performed as P-wave measurements according to the free-free resonant column method. The P-wave is the axial wave through the cylindrical specimen. In this case, the P-wave has been correlated against the shear strength of the stabilized material. Coupling between the P-wave and the E-module of the material, i.e. E0 (small strain levels), see Equation \ref{eq1}.

\begin{equation} \label{eq1}
	E_0=\rho \nu{_p2}=\rho(2L fL)2
\end{equation}

For the dimensioning of K/C columns, the shear strength of the material is used. This means that the different binder recipes are assessed according to the shear strength obtained. The connection between P-wave velocity and compressive strength is indirect and material dependent, i.e., depends on the composition of the soil material. A correlation between P-wave velocity and shear strength of the stabilized soil is reported in Figure \ref{Fig:09}.

\section{Discussion}
The laboratory testing showed a high repeatability between trials. The spread in compressive strength/shear strength increased slightly with increased strength, which is expected as minor inaccuracies in the test specimens have a greater effect. 

The requirements for the K/C columns in situ are a minimum shear strength of 150 kPa after 28 days. To ensure this, the requirement for the laboratory packed samples is set to at least 200 kPa. Several of the mixtures that contained both cement, quicklime and LKD met the requirements of at least 200 kPa after 28 days of storage. At a storage temperature of 7\degree, final strength is reached at 80 days (560 degree days) at the earliest. 

For the jet injection, the in situ requirement is a compressive strength of 2 MPa after 28 days. For laboratory-made samples, the requirement is set to at least 3 MPa. Binder recipes A and B passed the requirement of at least 3 MPa after 28 days even with a mixture of 58\% clay. For binder recipe C, the mean compressive strength was 2.77 MPa with a clay mix of 58\%.
